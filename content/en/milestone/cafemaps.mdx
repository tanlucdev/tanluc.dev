## Why I built it

CafeMaps started from a very ordinary need: finding a cafe that is quiet enough to work from, comfortable enough to stay for a while, and easy to evaluate without reading scattered reviews.

I wanted to turn that habit into a focused utility. Instead of browsing disconnected lists, people can scan cafes on a map, filter by work-friendly needs, and open the details only when a place looks promising.

## Approach

I kept the interface minimal, calm, and easy to scan. The map carries the main experience, the cafe list supports quick comparison, and the detail page gives just enough context to decide.

- Filter cafes by area and work-friendly criteria.
- Use compact cards for quick comparison.
- Keep cafe data manageable through a small CMS flow.

## What I learned

CafeMaps reminded me that a useful product does not need to be large. A small tool can still feel meaningful when it fits the context well.

The most interesting part was balancing map, list, and detail. When every surface asks for attention, the experience slows down. When each piece has a clear job, the whole product feels lighter.
